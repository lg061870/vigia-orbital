Now let’s look under the hood. What did the satellites see on the eve of the forecast? Three indicators, all from NASA’s MODIS satellites. NDVI measures greenness: in Liberia it fell from 0.82 in October to 0.64, as the dry season set in, slightly below normal for mid-January. NDMI measures the water in the leaves: it dropped from 0.29 to 0.10, so the vegetation is drying out fast. NBR is the burn index: it’s at its normal level, so no recent burn scar. Each index is a simple ratio of two light bands the satellite measures: near-infrared, which healthy leaves reflect strongly, and red or shortwave infrared, which change with chlorophyll and water. MODIS bands: Red = band 1, NIR = band 2, SWIR1 = band 6, SWIR2 = band 7. Every value is computed per pixel, only from clear (cloud-free) pixels, and averaged over the canton. Sources: MODIS MOD13Q1 and MOD09A1 v061 via Microsoft Planetary Computer.
