# Vigía Orbital: narration for the proof-first deck

Written in the spirit of the great science narrators: calm wonder, plain words, long breaths. First person: "I". About 520 words: close to 4 minutes at an unhurried pace. Every number comes from `docs/briefing.md`.

## Instruction to paste before each part

> Read the following text aloud exactly as written. Do not add any introduction, comment or closing words. Voice: a warm, thoughtful science narrator, in the manner of a classic documentary about the cosmos. Slow and unhurried, with a sense of wonder. Pause where you see "…". Give gentle weight to the words in CAPITALS.

## The ten parts (slide numbers of the proof-first deck)

| File | Slide | Text to read |
|---|---|---|
| `1` | 1 · Demo week | Far above us, in the silence of orbit, machines are watching the Earth… every day… and they remember everything. Let us travel back, to one Thursday evening. January, 2018. Liberia, Costa Rica. It has just rained. And a forecast says the danger of fire will climb… day by day… to HIGH, by Tuesday. The model that made this forecast had never seen that year. |
| `2` | 2 · Tuesday 23 | Tuesday came. And from orbit, NASA's satellites saw it: fire, in Liberia. The forecast had said thirty-nine percent. Not a certainty. A probability. Rain had lowered the odds… dry leaves, and a fire nearby, had raised them again. |
| `3` | 3 · Does it work? | But one good week could be luck. So I repeated the test… for every fire season since 2013. Each time, I hid one whole season from the model. It learned from all the others… and then had to forecast the season it had never seen. I compared it with the simplest forecast there is: assume this year will be like an average year. The model came out ahead… in twelve seasons out of fourteen. In the WORST fire seasons, it was up to twenty-one percent more accurate. And when it said ninety percent or more… fire came, ninety-six times in a hundred. One honest note: in 2026, the margin was almost nothing. A tie. |
| `4` | 4 · Harmonization | Before any forecast, there was a harder problem. Four satellites have watched these fires… and each sees the world differently. One sees up to FIVE times more fires than another. Add a satellite, and the numbers leap… though the fires have not changed. So I chose one witness at a time. And I counted not the sparks… but the DAYS with fire. The newest satellite agrees with the old one within four percent. The record… is unbroken. |
| `5` | 5 · The model | Now, the equation. It looks formidable. It is not. Read it… as a conversation. It begins with MEMORY: what usually happens here, in this canton, in this week of the year. Then it listens to the present. Is the weather drier than normal? Are the leaves thirstier? Has fire been seen nearby? Each answer nudges the odds… up, or down. And that curved symbol, sigma, simply folds it all into one number between zero and one. A probability. Nothing more mysterious than that. |
| `6` | 6 · The math is real | And the numbers in that equation were not chosen by me. They were LEARNED… from fourteen seasons of fire. Drier weather than normal multiplies the odds by one point seven. Every step, from raw NASA data to that number, lives in one open notebook. Run it yourself. You will find the same numbers I did. |
| `7` | 7 · Check it yourself | Science asks us not to believe… but to check. So everything here is open. A website, where you can choose any canton, any date since 2012, and see what the forecast said… and what truly happened. An open service that answers on request… even as a simple text message, small enough for a single bar of signal. |
| `8` | 8 · Impact | Why does it matter? Because Guanacaste holds sixty-three percent of Costa Rica's fire detections… most of them in three short months. Five days of warning… is the difference between arriving after the fire… and waiting for it. |
| `9` | 9 · Limits | It is not perfect. A light rain can fool it. Small fires hide beneath the clouds. And it has yet to be tested against the reports of the brigades themselves. That… is the next step. |
| `10` | 10 · Today | And so we return to today. The rains still fall, and the risk is low. But the dry season will come… as it always does. This time, we will see it coming. Vigía Orbital. NASA data… turned into five days of warning. |
