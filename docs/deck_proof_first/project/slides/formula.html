The risk of fire (machine learning model)

One logistic model per day ahead (k = 1 … 5)

RISKk(x, t) = σ( β₀ + β₁·Hk(x, t) + β₂·C(x, t) + β₃·V(x, t) + β₄·F(x, t) + β₅·T(x) )

Link function

σ(z) = 1 / (1 + e−z)

turns any score z into a probability between 0 and 1

z = β₀ + β₁Hk + β₂C + β₃V + β₄F + β₅T

Model terms

TermMeaning (x = canton, t = forecast day)
Hk(x, t)History: log-odds of fire on day t+k for this canton and week of the year, 2012–2025 (“a normal year”)
C(x, t)Weather vs normal: days without rain, max temperature, wind, humidity, soil moisture, 30-day rain (NASA POWER)
V(x, t)Vegetation vs normal: NDVI and NDMI (NASA MODIS)
F(x, t)Recent fire: detected in the canton in the last 7 days (NASA FIRMS)
T(x)Terrain: mean slope of the canton (NASADEM)
β₀ … β₅Coefficients, estimated from 2012–2025 by maximum likelihood, one set per day ahead

Result

RISKk(x, t) ∈ [0, 1]

probability that satellites detect fire in canton x on day t + k, using only what is known on day t

Here is the full formula. For each day ahead, k from one to five, the risk is the logistic function sigma of a weighted sum. H is history: the log-odds of fire on that day for this canton and week of the year, from 2012 to 2025 — our “normal year”. C is the weather compared with normal: days without rain, maximum temperature, wind, humidity, soil moisture and rain over the last 30 days, from NASA POWER. V is the vegetation compared with normal: NDVI and NDMI from MODIS. F is recent fire: whether the satellites detected fire in the canton in the last seven days. T is terrain: the canton’s mean slope from NASADEM. The betas are estimated from 2012 to 2025 by maximum likelihood, with one set for each day ahead. The result is a number between zero and one: the probability that satellites detect fire in that canton on that day, using only what we know the evening before. All inputs except H and F are standardized against normal. NBR is shown as a burn-scar indicator but is not a model input.
