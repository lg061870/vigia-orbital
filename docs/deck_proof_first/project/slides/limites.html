Limits and next steps

What we know it can’t do yet, and how we’ll get there

Honest limits

  • A light rain can make it too optimistic for a few days (Liberia, May 2024).
  • MODIS vegetation products arrive weeks late; weather arrives in days.
  • Canton level only: at this scale, terrain adds nothing.
  • “Fire” means a satellite detection: small fires under clouds are missed.
  • Not yet checked against SINAC field reports; 2026 test was a tie.

Next steps

  • Minimum-risk rule while vegetation stays dry.
  • Daily VIIRS reflectance for fresher vegetation data.
  • Forecasts on 400 m hexagons (grid and data already built).
  • Add the Canadian Fire Weather Index (FWI).
  • Conservation-area view for SINAC planning.
  • Validate with SINAC burned-area reports and IMN weather; pilot with one brigade.
No model is perfect, and we want to be clear about ours. A light rain can make it too optimistic for a few days — that’s what happened in Liberia in May 2024. MODIS vegetation products arrive weeks late. It works at canton level, where terrain adds nothing. Fire means a satellite detection, so small fires under clouds are missed. And we haven’t yet compared it with SINAC’s field reports; on the 2026 test season it tied with the average year. Next steps: a minimum-risk rule while vegetation stays dry; daily VIIRS reflectance for fresher vegetation; forecasts on 400-metre hexagons, for which the grid and data are already built; the Canadian Fire Weather Index; a conservation-area view for SINAC; and validation with SINAC and the national weather service, ideally piloting with one brigade.
