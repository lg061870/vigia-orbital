These are the estimated coefficients of the main model, which forecasts fire in the next five days. Briefly: the intercept is close to zero, so when everything is normal, the risk is just the normal year. History has a coefficient close to one: the model starts from what usually happens in that canton at that time of year. Weather drier than normal raises the odds by 68 percent per standard deviation; vegetation greener than normal lowers them by 21 percent; fire in the last week raises them by 16 percent; and terrain adds nothing at canton level. On seasons it never saw, the model reaches an AUC of 0.932, against 0.919 for the average year, and its probabilities are 8.4 percent more accurate. Technical notes: standard errors and z are derived from the 95% confidence intervals of the statsmodels fit (notebook C19); they are optimistic because consecutive days overlap, which is why we rely on season-by-season validation. Accuracy, recall and F1 are not reported because the model outputs probabilities, evaluated with AUC and Brier skill. The five lead-day models have the same structure (coefficients in the notebook, C26).
