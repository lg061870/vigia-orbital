# Vigía Orbital: video script, version 2 (English, about 3 minutes)

Written to be read aloud by an AI voice (or a person). First person: "I". Every number comes from `docs/briefing.md`.

## Instruction to paste before each part

> Read the following text aloud exactly as written. Do not add any introduction, comment or closing words. Voice: a confident, warm documentary narrator. Unhurried. Take a short pause where you see "…", and give a little extra weight to the words in CAPITALS.

## The nine parts

| File name | On screen | Text to read |
|---|---|---|
| `1` | Slide 1, today's forecast | What if you could check the wildfire forecast… the way you check the weather? This is Vigía Orbital. Five days of fire risk for Guanacaste, Costa Rica. Today, the risk is low: the rains are not over yet. So… let's turn back the clock. |
| `2` | Slide 2, demo week | January, 2018. Thursday evening. It has just rained, so Friday looks calm. Moderate. But watch the land dry out. Saturday… Sunday… Monday… and by Tuesday, the risk is HIGH. And remember: the model had never seen 2018. |
| `3` | Slide 3, Tuesday 23 | So what happened that Tuesday? NASA's satellites detected fire in Liberia. The forecast said thirty-nine percent. The rain had pulled the risk down… but dry vegetation, and a fire nearby that week, pushed it back up. |
| `site` | The website, then the text message | And this is not a mock-up. It is live. Open the site, pick a canton, pick a day… and it tells you WHY. Go back to any date since 2012. Behind it, an open service computes each forecast on request… and can send it as a plain text message. Under one kilobyte. Made for a brigade with one bar of signal. |
| `6` | Slide 6, harmonization | Now, the hard part. Four satellites have watched these fires… and they do not agree. VIIRS sees up to FIVE times more fires than MODIS. And every new satellite makes the numbers jump. More satellites… not more fire. My answer: one reference satellite at a time. And I count the DAYS with fire… not the detections. The newest satellite matches the old one within four percent. So the record… continues. |
| `7` | Slide 7, formula, then slide 8 at "Then it asks" | The model is simple on purpose. It starts with a normal year: this canton, this week. Then it asks three questions. Is the weather drier than normal? Is the vegetation drier? Has there been fire nearby? Drier weather alone… multiplies the odds by one point seven. |
| `9` | Slide 9, validation | Does it work? I tested it on every season since 2013… each one forecast blind. It beats an average year by eight percent. In the WORST seasons… by twenty-one. And when it says ninety percent or more… fire happens ninety-six times out of a hundred. One honest note: in 2026, it only tied. |
| `10` | Slide 10, impact | Why does this matter? Guanacaste holds sixty-three percent of Costa Rica's fire detections… most of them in just three months. Five days of warning means crews in the right place… BEFORE the fire. Not after. |
| `12` | Slide 12, notebook and QR | It is not perfect. Next, I want to test it against the field reports of the fire brigades. But everything is open: the data, the code, every number you just saw. Vigía Orbital. NASA data… turned into five days of warning. |

About 400 words: close to 3 minutes with the pauses.

## Recording checklist (OBS)
- Record desktop audio only; mute the microphone source.
- One recording per part, named as in the table (`1.mkv`, `site.mkv`, …), or one long recording with two seconds of silence between parts.
- If the voice adds any words that are not in the text, record that part again.
